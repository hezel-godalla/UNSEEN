THE DATA · MEN

Men and unpaid care.

Time-use data shows how care is shared across women and men, and why shared responsibility needs to include planning as well as tasks.

More than 3×

as much unpaid care work was performed by women as by men

In the ILO analysis, women performed 76.2% of total unpaid care-work hours across 64 countries.

Women 76.2% Men 23.8%*

*Calculated as the remainder of the women-men split; percentages are rounded.

SHARE THE WHOLE JOB

Doing a task is only one part.

Fairer sharing includes noticing what needs doing, planning it, remembering it, and following up. A task is not fully shared if one person still has to manage the whole process.

READ WITH CARE

Patterns vary by place and household.

The ILO figure is an aggregate across the countries included in its analysis. It describes a broad gender pattern, not the contribution of every man or the arrangements in every family.

Source International Labour Organization, “Care work and care jobs for the future of decent work” (2018) ILO reports women performed 76.2% of total unpaid care-work hours, more than three times as much as men, based on time-use data from 64 countries.
