PHILIPPINE CONTEXT

Invisible work in Filipino homes.

Mental load is not only the chore we can see. It includes remembering, planning, anticipating needs, coordinating people, and checking that responsibilities are completed.

VISIBLE TASK

Cooking dinner

HIDDEN MENTAL LOAD

Deciding what to cook, checking ingredients, considering the budget, remembering preferences, shopping, timing preparation, and planning for the next meal.

FAMILY, CULTURE & REALITY

There is no single Filipino household.

Responsibilities may be shared among parents, children, grandparents, siblings, relatives, and other caregivers. Work schedules, income, commuting, migration, household size, disability, age, and access to services can all affect how care is organized.

Gender expectations can also shape assumptions about who should cook, clean, organize, earn, or provide care. The goal of this project is not to blame one group, but to make responsibilities easier to notice and discuss.

REFLECTION

Ask who owns the whole process.

  • Who notices that something needs to be done?
  • Who remembers the deadline or schedule?
  • Who plans the steps?
  • Who does the visible task?
  • Who follows up when something changes?
WHY IT MATTERS

Invisible responsibilities can affect more than chores.

Time

Planning and caregiving can continue outside paid work or school hours and can reduce time for rest and personal activities.

Study

Students who also carry household or caregiving responsibilities may have to coordinate schoolwork with family needs.

Opportunity

Care responsibilities can influence when and how a person can participate in paid work, training, or community activities.

Fairness

Naming the planning behind a task makes it easier to discuss whether responsibility is being shared in a sustainable way.
